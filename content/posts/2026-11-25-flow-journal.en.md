## Background: the Jensen Huang effect lasted exactly one day

- In the US session on Nov 24 (local time), the **Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.88% (8,832.50)**, with all 30 members down. The Nasdaq lost 1.48% and the S&P 500 0.50%, while the Dow rose 0.85% to top 50,000.
- **Nvidia fell 5.86%** a day after its 31.5% surge, **Micron −11.01%**, **SK hynix ADR −9.90%**. BNP Paribas cut Micron to Neutral and slashed its target from $1,700 to $775, citing uncertainty in 2028 data-center spending plans and memory oversupply risk.
- In Seoul, Samsung was already down 4%+ and SK hynix 5%+ in Nextrade's 8 a.m. pre-market, and a **sell-side sidecar** hit eight minutes after the open. Yesterday it was a buy-side sidecar.

## What changed today: chips collapsed, the whole market did not

In the past, a big Nasdaq chip sell-off meant KOSPI fell across the board the next day. **Not today.**

- **Only chips and the Samsung group collapsed.** Samsung Electro-Mechanics −12.00%, SK Square −11.19%, Samsung Life −10.93%, Samsung C&T −8.29% — names tied to chip holdings or chip businesses fell around 10%.
- **Everything else was a modest pullback after yesterday's surge.** LG Energy Solution −1.91%, Hyundai Motor −2.00%, KB Financial −2.18% — about a third of the index drop.
- **Some sectors even rose.** Samsung Biologics +5.66%, Celltrion +3.83%, Alteogen +6.20% on KOSDAQ. Part of the money leaving chips rotated into biotech and healthcare — the same pattern as in the US, where chips crashed but the Dow and healthcare rose.
- **The −6% index move is about concentration.** Samsung and SK hynix make up about 45% of KOSPI's market cap. When both fall 10%, the index loses 4–5 points from them alone even if everything else holds. Decliners (595) outnumbered advancers (294) two to one, but the typical stock fell far less than the index.

In short, the rule is shifting from **"Nasdaq chips down = all of KOSPI down" to "Nasdaq chips down = KOSPI chips down."** Reading the whole market off the index alone can mislead you on days like this.

## Topic 1. Trading until 8 p.m. — the side effects keep showing

After today's close, stocks trading on Nextrade were also slipping below their regular-session closes (as of writing). Yesterday, Samsung rose 6.70% in the regular session but gave most of it back to +1.3% by the 8 p.m. combined close ([Nov 24 journal](/posts/2026-11-24-flow-journal/)). The recurring side effects of the longer evening session:

- **It overlaps head-on with the US pre-market.** US pre-market opens at 4 a.m. New York time — 6 p.m. in Seoul. From 6 to 8 p.m., Korean after-market prices move in real time with Nasdaq futures and US pre-market trading, and that carries into the next day's open. The Korean session effectively becomes a trailer for Wall Street.
- **Thin liquidity makes prices jump.** On the first day of the KRX after-market in September, static and dynamic volatility interruptions (VI) fired **1,637 times**. Over the summer, SK hynix even printed at its lower limit on Nextrade's pre-market on a trade of a single share.
- **Disclosures stop at 6 p.m., trading runs to 8.** News after 6 p.m. circulates only through articles or rumors, and retail investors trade two hours on price alone.

## Topic 2. The futures side wins too much — Binance as foreigners' playground

- Since June, Binance has offered 24-hour **perpetual futures on Samsung Electronics (SAMSUNGUSDT), SK hynix (SKHYNIXUSDT) and Hyundai Motor**, with **up to 50x leverage** on Samsung and SK hynix. You can hold a short position overnight — the same effect as short selling — without owning a single share.
- One analysis put trading in SK hynix-tracking perpetuals at **about 7% of actual cash-market turnover**. A separate report found a **0.98 correlation** between Hyperliquid's SK hynix perp move after the Korean close and the stock's next-day opening move. Price is effectively set first on offshore crypto exchanges, and the Korean market follows.
- Foreign and offshore money can trade Korean large caps overnight at high leverage, while Korean exchanges can't list such products and Korean retail can only reach them through unprotected workarounds. **Same stock, two markets with different rules** — and the edge goes to offshore leveraged money. On a day when chips move 10%, the biggest winner isn't the retail investor holding shares; it's whoever was short the perps.

## Topic 3. Policy extended the hours; the safeguards didn't follow

This is my opinion.

- **Trading hours now run 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.**, but disclosure hours, volatility controls and measures for the information gap haven't kept pace. Prices swinging on thin order books for four and a half hours after the close are effectively left alone.
- **On offshore exchanges' Korean-stock futures, regulators have done little beyond issuing a "caution" notice.** Products that influence Korean price discovery trade abroad at 50x, with no monitoring or tools to respond.
- The result: **Korean investors now face foreign and offshore futures money for longer hours, on worse terms.** Before extending hours further, the priority should be stopping price-setting power from moving offshore.

## Interpretation

**1. The Jensen Huang rally was a short squeeze, reversed in a day.** Buy sidecar yesterday, sell sidecar today. Foreigners sold more than ₩2T again, and chips gave back all of yesterday's gains.

**2. This drop is about the chip cycle.** The reason shifted from Jensen Huang's personal risk to memory oversupply and doubts over AI investment returns. Micron's target being cut in half feeds straight into Samsung and SK hynix.

**3. KOSPI is now close to a "chip index."** With two stocks at 45% of the market, the index and how the market actually feels have diverged. Non-chip names merely paused after yesterday's surge, and money flowed into biotech.

**4. Only individuals bought — ₩1.84T.** Retail absorbed the foreign selling while institutions sat out. Whether retail buying lasts long enough to stop the slide is still unknown.
